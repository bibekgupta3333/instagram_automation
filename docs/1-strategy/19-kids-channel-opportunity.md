# 19 — Kids channel opportunity and revenue experiment

> Research checked 2026-10-17. Five agents covered demand, monetisation, original concepts, production fit and rights. This is a proposed pilot, not an earnings promise or a launched channel.

## Decision

Test **Pocket Meadow Question Club**: original illustrated discovery stories for ages 3–5, watched with caregivers. Start with narrated stories; add original songs only after the storytelling and production process work. [Brand and episodes](../2-channels/20-pocket-meadow-question-club.md) · [Production and publishing requirements](../4-production/21-kids-pilot-production.md).

The business hypothesis is that useful, enjoyable evergreen episodes can build a returning audience and eventually earn YouTube advertising revenue. Neither revenue nor distribution is guaranteed. There is no verified automated playbook that guarantees a new channel one billion daily views.

| Primary evidence | Period and scope | What it supports |

One billion views per day sustained for 30 days means **30 billion monthly views**. For scale, dividing Moonbug's reported 4-billion monthly benchmark by 30 gives about 133 million daily views; that arithmetic is not a measured daily count. No source above establishes one billion launch-day views or likely performance for this project.

## Advertising economics

Kids content can earn contextual advertising revenue. Personalised advertising and remarketing are prohibited on made-for-kids content. Do not import the adult portfolio's estimated RPMs into this experiment. [YouTube advertising rules](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9713557?hl=en).

As checked today, ad-revenue YPP entry requires 1,000 subscribers plus either 4,000 qualified public watch hours in 12 months or 10 million qualified Shorts views in 90 days, followed by review and other eligibility requirements. Shorts Feed watch hours do not count toward the watch-hour route. [Current YPP eligibility](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851?hl=en).

YouTube has announced **February 1, 2027** entry thresholds of 1,000 subscribers plus 8,000 qualified watch hours in 365 days or 20 million qualified Shorts views in 90 days. It also announces a continuing 10-million/90-day minimum for monthly Shorts Creator Pool earnings. Recheck the official page and Studio before forecasting a launch across that date. [Official changes](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12843009).

Budget creator advertising revenue as **$0 before acceptance and activation of the relevant monetisation features**. For later sensitivity analysis, use `revenue = views / 1,000 × measured advertising revenue per 1,000 views`. Keep Shorts and watch-page earnings separate; total YouTube RPM may include income beyond ads.

| Hypothetical monthly watch-page views | At assumed $0.25 ad revenue / 1,000 views | At assumed $1.00 | At assumed $2.00 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100,000 | $25 | $100 | $200 |
| 1,000,000 | $250 | $1,000 | $2,000 |

These rates are deliberately illustrative inputs, **not observed kids-channel rates or predictions**. Subtract text-model costs, assets/music, review time and other expenses to calculate profit. Instagram/Facebook reach is not YouTube AdSense revenue; budget their revenue at zero until account-specific eligibility and actual earnings are established.

## Validation before expansion

1. **Week 1, local only:** produce one 45–60-second illustrated story. Obtain structured feedback from five consenting adult caregivers and an early-childhood educator; do not collect children's identities or recordings. Set a proposed cash cap of $50 and log human hours.
2. **Week 2:** revise the prototype and storyboard two genuinely different 3–4-minute episodes. Check character continuity, factual demonstrations and rights. This is additional production work, not a claim the current renderer handles a children's show automatically.
3. **Public experiment only after publishing support is deliberately implemented and reviewed:** release six original episodes over six weeks, accurately classified as made for kids. No automatic posting is authorised by this document. Use adult-directed behind-the-scenes posts for Instagram/Facebook, assessed separately for their actual audience.
4. Compare episode completion, average percentage viewed, returning viewers and production hours within the same format. Comments are unavailable on made-for-kids videos; collect feedback from adults separately. Do not buy traffic or incentivise repeated viewing.
5. Proposed continuation rule: at least four of five caregiver reviewers find the learning objective clear; no unresolved educator/rights concerns; the final three public episodes improve median percentage viewed and views per production hour over the first three after equal observation windows. These are chosen experiment rules, not industry benchmarks. Small samples require further testing before expansion.

An alternative is an adult-directed channel demonstrating how caregivers can teach everyday science. It fits original screen/hands-only demonstrations, but is a different audience hypothesis; calling a preschool cartoon "for parents" does not change its classification. Do not assume higher advertising yield without measured evidence.
