# Rank Test Lab — 20 evergreen experiment treatments

These are detailed treatments, not complete word-for-word narration. All 20 experiments are proposed and **not yet performed**. No ranking chart, outcome, date, traffic gain or business result is asserted. The planned reveals explain what a valid observation could establish; an eventual measured result must replace the conditional outline before a results episode is filmed.

Use an independently hosted, channel-owned controlled test site containing useful, original small-business reference material, clearly identified as a lab. No customer sites, scraped competitors, fake businesses, fabricated reviews, bought links or automated searches. Technical duplicate variants remain a small, bounded diagnostic set, not a scaled page campaign. No protocol below authorizes deployment or publishing.

## Common experiment and production rules

Before any future experiment, register the hypothesis, primary endpoint, eligibility, assignment, exclusions, observation window and minimum useful sample. Default planning window: 28 baseline days and 42 observation days, subject to feasibility review before launch, never extended merely to find a favorable result. For discovery tests, start the clock at documented exposure, report non-events as censored and never turn a missing observation into a zero. Repeat across matched, genuinely different topic pages where feasible; randomly assign within topic/age/baseline bands. Sitewide interference and small samples limit causal conclusions. Keep other deployments frozen, log external changes and separate experiments into nonoverlapping cohorts or observation periods. No live search split test that serves different content to crawlers and people.

Keep full denominators, query/device/country filters, excluded data, null outcomes and uncertainty. Search impressions, indexing, clicks, task completion and revenue are different endpoints. Never manufacture searches or clicks to generate evidence. If there is insufficient organic data, publish a protocol demonstration explicitly labeled as such, not a result episode. Local demonstrations establish only what was demonstrated locally.

All graphs, maps, overlays and interface diagrams are planned manual graphics; their animation or interactivity is not a claim about current renderer support. Use owned page captures and measurements, original tabletop footage and generic unbranded diagrams. Do not reproduce unlicensed software dashboards. Any necessary permitted account capture must hide identifiers and document permissions. Teal/chalk/orange follows the brand palette. Thumbnail variants each have one focal object, zero to three manually added words, and no AI-generated text, logos or famous faces. Use an AI voice disclosure, adult audience classification and no implied Google affiliation. No affiliate placements are proposed.

## C10-01 — Can a Title Say What the Page Actually Does?

**Format & length:** 16:9, 8:00; independent 35-second vertical.

**Evergreen angle:** Does task specificity change search presentation? Refresh after title-guidance changes.

**Title & thumbnail A/B:** A: “A Clearer Title, Same Page?”; one paper tab centered in fog, “CLEARER?”. B: “Testing the Task-First Title”; one embossed label viewed edge-on, “SAME PAGE”.

**Hook (0:00–0:10):** “This page has two possible names. Only one tells you what you can do here.” Macro: reversible title card.

**Attention magnet:** Will the submitted title even appear? Answer the observation rules at 6:15.

**Story beats:** 0:00–1:20, comparison structure: show a packaging guide’s vague title. 1:20–3:00, hypothesis: adding the specific task improves task-matched presentation; randomly assign matched guides, retaining unchanged controls. VO: “The article stays still while its label changes.” Shoot a locked page with moving title strip. 3:00–4:45, evidence plan: archive source titles, observed title links and query cohorts. 4:45–6:15, complication: Google may display different wording; query mix can change. VO: “A better click rate can arrive with a different audience.” Show denominator cards. 6:15–8:00, reveal the conditional title-adoption and click-through comparison; a rewrite or sparse sample can prevent attribution.

**Payoff & final line:** Answer whether adoption and comparable-query observations support the hypothesis. “A clearer label earns a test, not a victory lap.”

**Short cut:** Hook with the reversible card; show unchanged body evidence; answer why title adoption must precede interpretation.

**Evidence & rights:** Not yet performed. Verify G1/G2/G5; measure displayed-title adoption and query-stratified click-through, with impressions reported. Confounder: query composition. Retain owned captures and assignment log.
