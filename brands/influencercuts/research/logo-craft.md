# InfluencerCuts logo craft research

Date: 2026-11-16. Scope: current logo practice (2025-2026) for a fan-made YouTube Shorts channel that posts short captioned cuts of streamer and influencer moments. The purpose is to extract principles. Nothing here is to be copied; section 6 lists marks the logo must stay away from.

Reliability key used throughout:

- **[fetched]** I opened the page and the statement comes from its text.
- **[snippet]** The statement comes from a search-result summary; the page itself was not opened.
- **[unverified]** From memory or from weak/conflicting sources. Check before relying on it.
- **[own calc]** My own arithmetic (WCAG 2.x relative-luminance contrast), not a source claim.

Limits of this research: I read article text, not images. Dribbble, Behance and Brand New pages were reached only as text or index listings, so no gallery image was visually inspected. Descriptions of what existing logos look like come from written descriptions.

## 2. Findings

Each finding is tied to the source that supports it.

**F1. Negative space is now designed on purpose, and the gap is often the focal point.**
LogoLounge 2026: "Negative space isn't just a byproduct, it's being actively recruited", and of its "Open Axis" trend (radial forms whose elements nearly touch), "That sliver of negative space isn't accidental; it's the point." [fetched, S1]
Implication: a thin, deliberate gap between two solid masses is a current, credible device. For a channel about cuts, the gap can be the cut.

**F2. Three workable negative-space techniques: between letters, symbol-in-symbol, and inside one letterform.**
Dribbble Stories groups examples this way and advises that the hidden image be relevant to the product and easy to find once noticed. [fetched, S4]
Implication: the "inside a letterform" route (keep the letter's solid shape intact, carve one relevant idea into it) suits a monogram used at avatar size better than a hidden shape between several letters.

**F3. Simple geometric kits and angled blocks are current; monograms are being assembled from few parts.**
LogoLounge 2026 "Colorforms": simple geometric pieces with open spacing, often forming monograms; "Slash Bar": angled parallelograms used as modular blocks that create direction. The report's summary line: "a stripped-down kit of geometric parts, assembled with intention but never overworked." [fetched, S1]
Implication: build the mark from two to four solid pieces. A slanted bar or parallelogram reads as direction and as a cut without drawing a tool.

**F4. The cut itself can be the terminal: deep, controlled cuts and stepped strokes carry meaning.**
LogoLounge 2025 "Sharps": deep dagger-like cuts treated as "purposeful termination points"; "Scalers": a progression of strokes that grow or step to suggest momentum; "Squared": the square used as a structural component meshed with type. [fetched, S2]
LogoLounge 2026 "Piano": stepped parallel lines revived from 1980s motion graphics; "Phasers": rotation shown as staged, visible steps. [fetched, S1]
Implication: a sequence of slices of different lengths can say "segments of a longer video" in a way that is specific to this channel.

**F5. Implied motion is the dominant theme of the 2026 report, even in still marks.**
"Movement, even when nothing is technically moving, is another clear throughline." [fetched, S1] The Dieline's coverage repeats that motion is the overarching theme. [fetched, intro only before the paywall; see the failed list in section 1]
Implication: a slant, offset or stagger gives a static avatar energy. It should come from the geometry, not from blur.

**F6. Gradients are in fashion at large sizes and fail at small sizes.**
LogoLounge 2025 lists "BlurTails" (soft gradient trails) and notes gradients evolving into one-sided shadows and layered ombre [fetched, S2]; the 2026 report says "gradients behave like frequencies" [fetched, S1]. Against that, the favicon guide says to use flat fills or at most a two-tone split [fetched, S5], and ManyPixels says fine linework, gradients and layered shadows "dissolve" at 24 to 40 px and that inverting a colour file for dark mode produces muddy midtones [fetched, S6].
Implication: the master mark must be flat. A gradient may exist only as an optional large-format banner treatment, never in the avatar.

**F7. Thin strokes and tight counters fail first; solid fills survive.**
Favicon guide: remove strokes thinner than about 2 px at final size, prefer filled shapes over outlines, keep counters open, and "push contrast further than looks comfortable on a large screen". [fetched, S5] Supporting snippet: open up inner corners slightly so they do not clog. [snippet, S18]
Worked consequence [own calc]: the YouTube avatar is uploaded at 800 x 800 and shown at about 98 px on a channel page and far smaller in comments (S9). A 2 px minimum at a 32 px render equals 50 px on the 800 px master, and 67 px if the comment avatar renders at 24 px. So no stroke or gap in the master should be thinner than roughly 50 to 70 px (6 to 8 percent of the canvas width).

**F8. The avatar is a circle, shown tiny, and words do not survive in it.**
ChannelPreview: upload 800 x 800 square, 2 MB maximum, displayed about 98 x 98 px on desktop and smaller in comments; corners are cropped by the circle; words become unreadable at around 32 px; use a simple mark, and use a bright background or sharp outline to separate from YouTube's dark mode. [fetched, S9] The favicon guide agrees: one glyph at most, "three or four letters squeezed into 16px are unreadable". [fetched, S5]
Implication: the avatar carries a single mark, not "InfluencerCuts" and not a two-letter "IC" unless the two letters fuse into one shape. The wordmark lives on the banner and watermark only.
Note: exact display sizes vary between third-party guides (98 px and 120 px both appear) and I could not confirm them on an official YouTube page. [unverified]

**F9. A mark that must sit on both dark and light backgrounds needs an opaque tile or a mid-tone colour, and neons need care.**
Favicon guide: a near-black mark on transparent fails in dark mode and a white mark fails in light mode; fix with an opaque brand-colour tile; palette of one dominant colour, one high-contrast neutral, one optional accent. [fetched, S5] DesignCrowd: bright brand colours can look over-saturated or glowing on dark backgrounds; avoid high-saturation neons unless the glow is intended; a shape container helps on both backgrounds. [fetched, S8]
Contrast figures for candidate and competitor colours [own calc; background #0F0F0F is my assumption for YouTube dark mode, unverified]:

| Colour | vs white | vs #0F0F0F | Note |
|--------|----------|------------|------|
| #FF0000 pure red | 4.00 | 4.79 | Works both ways, but owned by YouTube |
| #9146FF violet | 4.64 | 4.13 | Works both ways, but owned by Twitch |
| #53FC18 neon green | 1.37 | 13.97 | Disappears on white; associated with Kick |
| #25F4EE cyan | 1.38 | 13.92 | Disappears on white; associated with TikTok |
| #FF5A3C coral-orange | 3.10 | 6.19 | Passes 3:1 both ways; unclaimed in this category |
| #FF6A00 orange | 2.87 | 6.68 | Just under 3:1 on white |

Implication: use an opaque filled tile that itself passes 3:1 on both white and near-black, with a single high-contrast neutral mark on it. Coral-orange or cobalt are the open lanes. LogoLounge 2026 notes "green is having a moment (actually, more like a takeover)" [fetched, S1], which is a reason to avoid green here, apart from the Kick association.

**F10. Obvious category symbols describe the category, not the brand.**
ManyPixels: cliche symbols (leaves for sustainability, shields for security, swooshes for motion) make a logo look like its competitors and raise trademark risk. Fix: "take the obvious idea one step away from the cliche", then reverse-image-search and check the USPTO database. Trend test: if you can name five brands already using a look, it is a trend. [fetched, S6]
Implication: for a clips channel the category cliches are the play triangle, scissors, film strip, clapperboard and razor blade. The play triangle is also the core of the YouTube icon. All are out as the primary idea.

**F11. Grids serve proportion, and the eye overrules the grid.**
Inkbot: round letters such as C and O and pointed vertices need overshoot past the cap height and baseline or they look small; bounding-box centring can look off, so centre by perceived weight; review at deployment scale with guides hidden. [fetched, S7] Supporting snippets: a grid encodes ratios such as stroke weight to height so the mark scales without drift [snippet, S16]; vertical strokes look heavier than horizontal strokes of equal width, so they are thinned slightly [snippet, S17].
Implication: set one unit (the cut-gap width) and derive stroke weight, corner radius and padding from it. Then centre optically inside the circle crop; a mark with a slant will need to be nudged against the lean.

**F12. Design a small family, not one logo, and test it in real contexts.**
Creative Bloq (Jack Shaw): a logo that reads on an app icon or social avatar "needs a simpler shape" than the one used large; a small family sharing one idea keeps branding steady "across light and dark themes, small screens and fast moving content". [fetched, S3] ManyPixels: supply primary, horizontal, stacked and icon-only versions, plus all-black, all-white and single-colour; approve on a mock-up sheet (favicon, avatar, dark footer, over a photograph), not on a slide. [fetched, S6] StreamYard's own rebrand cut detail because the old illustration "didn't survive at small icon sizes" and unified everything on one brand colour. [fetched, S13]
Implication: deliverables are avatar mark, corner watermark for the videos (one colour, must sit over busy footage), and banner lockup with wordmark.

Secondary observation, weaker evidence: the Creative Bloq piece reports a reaction against "blandification" toward marks that are "playful, unexpected". [fetched, S3] A fan channel can afford more character than a platform brand.

## 3. Do

1. Build the avatar as one flat, solid mark on an opaque colour tile (F6, F9).
2. Keep every stroke and gap at or above about 6 to 8 percent of canvas width, 50 to 70 px on an 800 px master (F7).
3. Use at most three colours: one dominant, one high-contrast neutral, one optional accent (F9).
4. Choose a tile colour that reaches 3:1 against both white and near-black. Check the final hex with a contrast checker (F9).
5. Make the cut a deliberate gap or terminal in the form, so the idea is carried by negative space (F1, F4).
6. Assemble from two to four geometric parts on one unit grid, then correct optically (F3, F11).
7. Keep all critical art inside the inscribed circle, with roughly 10 to 12 percent padding, and centre by eye (F8, F11, S5).
8. Design the one-colour version first. If the idea needs colour to read, change the shape (F12, S6).
9. Test at 16, 24, 32, 48 and 98 px, on white, on near-black, and over a busy video frame (F7, F8, F12).
10. Produce a family: avatar mark, one-colour watermark, banner lockup with wordmark (F12).
11. Before release, run a reverse image search and a trademark database search on the final mark (F10).

## 4. Avoid

1. Gradients, glows, shadows or blur trails in the avatar or watermark (F6).
2. Outline-only or monoline marks, and thin strokes of any kind (F7).
3. The full name, or any word, inside the avatar (F8).
4. Play triangle, scissors, film strip, clapperboard or razor blade as the main idea (F10).
5. A rounded rectangle containing a triangle in any colour (YouTube).
6. Red with white, violet with white, neon green with black, cyan plus red-pink offsets on black (section 6).
7. Chromatic-offset or "glitch" duplication of the mark (TikTok).
8. Speech-bubble silhouettes and pixel-stepped letterforms (Twitch, Kick).
9. Angular interlocked strokes crossing on a diagonal in white on a black tile (CapCut).
10. Near-black or white marks on a transparent background (F9).
11. Internal cuts so fine they close up below 60 px. DesignRush reports exactly this failure for CapCut's mark, which "reads as a dense angular blob" at small sizes (S10).
12. Using any real streamer's face, likeness or channel branding in the logo. This is my recommendation for a fan channel, not a source finding.

## 5. Five original concept directions

Shared rules for all five: flat fills, one tile colour plus one neutral, no thin strokes, readable at 24 px, sits inside the circle crop. Suggested palette to test first: coral-orange tile (around #FF5A3C) with near-black ink (#14110F) and warm off-white (#FFF4E0); alternative lane is cobalt (around #2F5BFF) with off-white. These hex values are starting points from my contrast calculation, not validated brand colours.

**D1. "Split I" (monogram with the cut as the gap).**
A heavy slab capital I whose stem is severed by one diagonal gap, with the upper half slid sideways by one unit so the two halves no longer line up. The offset shows that a piece has been moved, which is what an edit does. Two solid pieces, one gap. Follows F1, F3, F5. Risk to manage: the gap must stay at or above 8 percent of canvas width; the offset must be large enough not to look like a rendering error.

**D2. "C with a take-out" (inside-a-letterform negative space).**
A thick geometric C (near three-quarter ring) from which a single parallelogram slice has been removed and re-set just outside the ring, at the mouth of the C, as a detached tab. The removed piece is the clip. Reads as C for Cuts and as "a piece taken out of something larger". Follows F2, F3, F4. Risk to manage: keep it clearly a ring segment, not a rounded "play" or "refresh" icon; apply overshoot so the C does not look small in the circle (F11).

**D3. "Three slices" (stepped segments, no letter).**
Three slanted solid bars of clearly different lengths (long, short, medium), stacked with equal gaps, leaning at one shared angle. It stands for a long stream reduced to short cuts, and echoes stacked caption lines. Follows F4 (Scalers, Piano) and F5. Risk to manage: three horizontal bars can read as a menu icon or an equaliser; the shared slant, unequal lengths and a ragged left edge are what prevent that, so test this one hardest.

**D4. "Caption block" (the channel's own signature feature as the mark).**
A solid rounded rectangle, like a single burned-in caption word tile, with one clean diagonal slice through it; the smaller part drops by one unit. Captioned cuts are what the channel makes, so the mark is specific to the product instead of to "video" in general. Follows F10 (one step away from the cliche) and F1. Risk to manage: must not carry a tail or notch (speech bubble, Twitch) and must not contain a triangle (YouTube); keep it a plain bar, wider than tall.

**D5. "IC ligature" (two letters fused into one shape).**
A heavy vertical bar (I) set tight inside the open mouth of a thick C, sharing one stroke weight, separated only by a uniform cut-gap that runs as a single continuous channel between them. At small size it reads as one emblem; at large size it resolves into I and C. Follows F3 (Colorforms monograms), F7 and F8. Risk to manage: a bar inside a ring resembles a power symbol or a "pause in a circle"; rotating the C's opening off-axis and squaring the C's terminals moves it away from both.

Suggested next step: draw all five in one colour at 800 px, reduce to 24 px, and discard any that fail before colour is discussed.

## 7. Could not verify

- Official YouTube avatar display sizes and the dark-mode background colour (#0F0F0F assumed). Third-party guides disagree (98 px vs 120 px; 2 MB vs 4 MB file limit).
- Official hex values for Twitch, TikTok, Kick and YouTube. Brand pages either would not load or carried no text.
- The visual appearance of any logo cited. No images were inspected.
- Brand New critiques. Only post titles and dates were readable (S14), so no finding rests on Brand New.
- Behance was not reached directly; one Behance project is linked from the Creative Bloq article (S3) but was not opened.
- LogoLounge trend descriptions are summaries produced from the report pages by a reading tool. The quoted sentences are as returned; I did not check them character by character against the page.
- A "54% of designers use negative space" figure that appeared in a search snippet (merehead.com) has no traceable origin and is not used.
- Whether any of the five directions collides with an existing registered mark. No trademark or reverse-image search has been done.
