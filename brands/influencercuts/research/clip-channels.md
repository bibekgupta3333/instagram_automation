# InfluencerCuts logo research: clip channels, streaming and short-form brands

Date: 2026-11-16. Purpose: extract principles for an original InfluencerCuts mark. Nothing here is to be copied; the "must not resemble" section exists to keep the mark clear of existing trademarks.

## Limits of this research (read first)

- **No logo images were viewed.** All descriptions of existing marks come from text on the pages below, not from looking at the artwork.
- **No individual clip or highlight channel's profile picture was inspected.** The YouTube read-only connector failed to start, and searches returned vendor branding guides, not analyses of specific clip channels. Statements about "what clip channels do" are therefore inferred from general channel-icon and streamer-branding guidance, and are marked as such.
- **No official brand guideline text was retrieved.** brand.twitch.com returned only a download link, brand.youtube returned only image file names, and the YouTube Help page returned no body text. Platform details below come from third-party sites.
- Most sources are design blogs, agency pages or vendor content. Treat their claims as practitioner opinion, not data.

## Findings

1. **The avatar is seen far smaller than it is designed.** Upload is 800 x 800 px, but the channel page shows it at about 98 px, search and subscriptions at about 48 to 52 px, the watch page at about 40 to 48 px, and comments at about 40 px, always in a circle. Sizes are approximate and vary by device. (S2; S1 agrees on 800 px and about 98 px.) For Shorts, the watch-page and comment sizes are the ones viewers meet most. That last sentence is my inference.

2. **Thin lines and edge text are the first things to fail.** vidIQ: "Design for thumbnail size, not full resolution"; keep the focal point centred with padding, avoid thin lines, use thick strokes and high contrast, and if the mark is not recognisable at fingernail size, remove detail first, then raise contrast, then add padding. (S1) Apple says the same from the platform side: avoid extremely thin line weights and sharp corners because they lose detail at small sizes. (S3)

3. **One idea, few shapes.** Apple: find one concept, "make it the core idea of your icon, and express it in a simple, unique way with a minimal number of shapes", on a simple solid or gradient background that does not fill the whole canvas. (S3) CapCut's channel-icon guide says the same for short-form feeds: one main idea (a face, initials, a symbol or a product shape), not a combination. (S4)

4. **Filled shapes beat outlines.** Apple's own do and don't example shows an outlined ring as incorrect and filled, overlapping solid shapes as correct. (S3) The esports guide adds a silhouette test: fill the mark solid black and it should still be recognisable. (S5)

5. **Text: a one-to-three character monogram at most.** Apple: include text only when essential; a first-letter mnemonic can help recognition, but words are too small to read and clutter the icon. (S3) CapCut: one to three large characters, drop wordmarks and taglines. (S4) "InfluencerCuts" as a word cannot appear in the avatar; "IC" can.

6. **A tight palette that survives dark, busy video.** Esports guidance: one primary colour, one strong neutral (black or white) and at most one accent, chosen to hold over dark stream and game footage, with a single-colour version that still works. Heavy gradient dependence is a warning sign. (S5) CapCut adds that contrast matters more than a fashionable palette, and that dark-on-dark or pale-on-muted marks vanish in the feed. (S4)

7. **Colour ownership is real, so borrowed platform colours read as the platform.** The Twitch article says the 2019 purple (#9146FF, replacing #6441A5) is now so tied to Twitch that the colour alone signals the brand. (S6) TikTok's cyan and fuchsia offset on black, Kick's neon green and YouTube's red work the same way. (S7, S9, S13) A fan channel that uses any of these will look like an official or affiliated property.

8. **The strongest platform marks compress two ideas into one shape.** Twitch's Glitch is a speech bubble (chat) with a blocky, pixel edge (games). (S6) TikTok's mark is a musical note that is also a lowercase "d" for Douyin, and its colour offset depicts tremolo, a "trembling sound". (S7) Both are a single silhouette, not a symbol plus a decoration.

9. **Ambiguity costs meaning; reach can hide that.** DesignRush describes CapCut's white angular symbol on a black rounded tile as legible but unclear: writers read it as a camera, scissors, tape ribbons, a film strip or a sliced X, and at about 60 px the internal cuts close up so most people see an "X". Its recognition comes from being stamped on exported clips by default, about 200 million monthly users by 2022. (S8) A small channel has no such reach, so its mark must be clear without help.

10. **The mark doubles as a clip watermark.** CapCut and Medal both overlay their logos on clips, and that distribution is what makes the mark familiar. (S8; Medal detail from search snippets, unverified.) Streamer-branding advice suggests a simplified, light, semi-transparent corner version about 80 to 120 px wide. (Eklipse, search snippet only, unverified.) The InfluencerCuts mark should have a one-colour version that reads in a video corner.

11. **Generic letters and unexplained symbols get rejected; a clear tie to the name is accepted.** Dexerto's list: Evil Geniuses dropped its crest for a plain font with no identity and later restored an adapted crest; FPX's letters felt random to outsiders; Excel's first redesign ignored the obvious "XL" link and its later logo, which used it, was much better received; HellRaisers' new mark was never explained. (S12) A monogram alone is not enough: the letters need a distinctive idea attached to the name.

12. **Design for a circle or shield envelope, and test early in the real slot.** Esports marks are fitted to a roughly circular or shield-shaped envelope so they crop cleanly into avatars; the avatar circle is called "the most punishing test". (S5) CapCut recommends previewing in a circular crop beside three recent thumbnails at phone size, and matching the icon palette to the video packaging, for example caption colours. (S4)

## Do

- Design at 40 to 48 px in a circular crop first, then scale up. (S1, S2)
- Use one idea and a minimal number of filled shapes. (S3, S4)
- Keep text to "IC" or no text. (S3, S4)
- Use thick strokes and high contrast; leave padding so the circle does not clip the mark. (S1)
- Use one primary colour, one neutral and at most one accent, with a single-colour version. (S5)
- Pass the solid-black silhouette test. (S5)
- Tie the mark visibly to the name: the "cut" should be readable in the shape. (S12)
- Fuse two ideas into one silhouette, not a symbol with a decoration beside it. (S6, S7)
- Make a one-colour corner watermark variant for the clips. (S8)
- Match the palette to the caption style used in the videos. (S4)
- Test beside three real Shorts first frames at phone size. (S4)

## Avoid

- The platform colours and pairings listed above. (S6, S7, S9, S13)
- Play triangles, speech bubbles, musical notes, clapperboards and pixel-block lettering as the main symbol. (table above)
- Thin outlines, fine detail, sharp slivers and gradients the mark depends on. (S1, S3, S5)
- The full channel name, taglines, or instruction words such as "Watch" or "Clips" inside the avatar. (S3)
- Photos or faces of streamers. Apple advises against photos at small sizes (S3); for a fan channel a real person's face would also imply endorsement. The second point is my judgement, not from a source.
- Plain initials with no idea attached. (S12)
- Internal cuts so narrow that they close up at 60 px and turn the mark into an "X" or a blob. (S8)
- Dark-on-dark or pale-on-muted colour pairs. (S4)

## Five original concept directions

Colour suggestion common to all (a suggestion, not a research finding): a warm caption yellow or amber on a deep ink navy, with off-white as the neutral. It stays clear of YouTube red, Twitch purple, Kick green and TikTok cyan and fuchsia, and it can match yellow burned-in captions. If yellow is used, compare against Snapchat first.

1. **Sliced monogram.** A heavy "IC" filling the circle, cut by one wide diagonal slice, with the upper part shifted a little along the slice. One colour on a solid ground. The gap must be wide enough to stay open at 40 px (finding 9). Follows findings 2, 5, 11.

2. **Trim handles.** The "I" is a solid vertical in-point bar and the "C" is a thick open bracket, like the in and out handles on an editing timeline, with a short solid block between them as the kept clip. Monogram and "cut" in one silhouette. Follows findings 3, 4, 8.

3. **Cut caption.** Two stacked rounded bars of unequal length, like a two-line burned-in caption, with the end of the upper bar sliced off at an angle and the offcut nudged away. Refers to "captioned cuts" with no letters. Risk: could read as a generic menu or text icon, so the angled offcut must be large. Follows findings 3, 6, 12.

4. **Notched frame.** A solid upright 9:16 slab with one corner sliced off and displaced, so the slab and the offcut suggest a "C" opening around an "I". Must not be red and must carry no play shape, to stay clear of Shorts. Follows findings 4, 7, 8.

5. **Cut waveform.** Five thick rounded vertical bars of varied height, like a moment's audio peak, with a clean diagonal gap removing a slice through the middle; the tallest bar stands as the "I". Limit to five bars so it survives at 40 px. Risk: waveforms are common in podcast and audio branding, so the cut has to be the dominant feature. Follows findings 2, 4, 11.

Recommended next step: draw all five as solid black silhouettes in a 48 px circle, discard any that fail the silhouette test, then compare the survivors by eye against the marks in the table above.
