# Retention Architecture

> **Studio adaptation.** Pipeline defaults (doc 06 §2-3, `studio/stages/script.py`):
> 150 wpm (not 145) times the brand voice speed; the hook lands in the first 1-2 s (about
> 5 words); one picture per beat every 2.5-4 s for Shorts; the cta is a loop line that ends
> mid-thought and replays into the hook. Loops and re-engagement beats are planning tools
> only; their names never appear in `text`. "Required visual" for a hook card means beat 1's
> `visual_prompt`, the frame on screen while the hook is spoken.

Retention is the consequence of fulfilled curiosity, clear progress, useful density, and credible payoff. Treat tactics as design hypotheses rather than universal laws.

## Hook design

The opening must do three jobs quickly:

1. Confirm the viewer clicked the correct video.
2. Make the payoff concrete.
3. Provide enough proof or tension to justify the next beat.

Avoid greetings, biography, vague superlatives, manufactured stakes, and claims the body cannot prove.

## Hook candidate card

```text
HOOK:
MECHANISM: payoff | tension | demonstration | story
PROMISE:
FIRST VISUAL:
CREDIBILITY REQUIRED:
FAILURE MODE:
```

## Loop ledger

Every open loop requires a named close point.

```text
LOOP ID | OPEN | VIEWER QUESTION | CLOSE BEAT | PAYOFF
```

Remove loops that only restate the title. Close the central loop before the CTA.

## Long-form beat grammar

Use only where natural:

```text
Re-engage → Evidence / story / demonstration → Explanation → Consequence → Bridge
```

A re-engagement beat should change information mode: claim to example, abstract to visual, problem to mechanism, expectation to counterexample. Repetitive verbal teasers do not count.

## Pacing

- Estimate runtime using the creator’s measured WPM; use 145 WPM only as a fallback.
- Increase visual change frequency when the information is abstract or screen-native.
- Slow down around unfamiliar mechanisms, high-stakes claims, and final synthesis.
- Preserve silence when it increases comprehension or emotional weight.
- Use channel retention curves to recalibrate future defaults.

## Shorts compression

For Shorts:

- Lead with payoff, consequence, or visible result.
- Remove setup that the visual already communicates.
- Use one mechanism and one proof beat.
- Close the loop before the final second.
- Prefer a loop-compatible ending only when it remains honest and comprehensible.

## CTA

Use one primary CTA. Earn it after value or proof. A watch-next CTA is often structurally stronger than generic subscription language because it continues the viewer’s current question.
